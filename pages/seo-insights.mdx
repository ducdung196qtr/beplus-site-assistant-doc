# SEO Insights

This is the tab that pays for itself. It reads your conversations and answers two
questions no analytics package can:

1. **What do people actually ask about?**
2. **What do they ask that your site has no page for?**

The second one is a list of pages to write, in your visitors' own words.

![SEO Insights tab](/img/07-conversations-seo.png)

## The button

One press of **Analyse** does all of it. There is no schedule and no background
job — **nothing is analysed unless you ask**.

That is a deliberate choice: analysis costs AI calls, and therefore money. It runs
when an administrator wants it, not continuously.

The button tells you what it will do before you press it — **"Analyse 12
conversations"**, or **"Analyse new conversations"** when there is nothing new.
A progress bar shows how far along it is.

## A conversation is only ever analysed once

Each conversation is stamped after it is analysed, so the next press looks only at
what has arrived since. **Pressing repeatedly costs nothing extra.** You cannot
run up a bill by being curious.

Closing the page halfway through is safe too — the next press carries on from
where it stopped rather than starting over.

## What the four cards mean

### What people ask about

The subjects visitors ask about, **grouped by meaning rather than wording**.

"How do I install it?", "steps to set it up" and "installation guide?" are one
topic with a count of three — not three separate one-off rows. This is what keeps
the report readable on a busy site, where hundreds of slightly different phrasings
would otherwise bury the signal.

> 💡 **Read this as a content backlog.**
> The most-asked subjects are the ones most deserving of a proper page, even if
> your site already mentions them somewhere.

### Questions the assistant could not answer

The most valuable card on the screen.

It lists the subjects visitors asked about that **your site has no page for** —
"Do you ship to Japan?", "What are your support hours?". Each row is a page worth
writing, quoting the visitor's own question.

This is honest by design. It excludes:

- Questions the assistant **did answer**
- Greetings and small talk
- Questions the assistant was **configured to refuse** — a refusal is a setting
  you chose, not a missing page

It works for questions asked in any language, so a site with international
visitors still gets a readable gap report.

### Top questions by topic

The detail behind the first card — the individual questions grouped under each
subject, so you can see the exact wording people used.

### Pages that generate the most questions

Which pages visitors were on when they started chatting. Counted directly from
the log **without the AI**, so it keeps updating even when the AI is unavailable,
and always reflects the true total rather than an analysis that has not run yet.

If a page generates a lot of questions, the page is either unclear or missing
something. Either way it is worth reading.

## The counters

Above the cards, three figures tell you how much of the picture you are seeing:

- How many conversations have been **analysed**
- How many are **still waiting**
- The **total**

If "waiting" is a large number, the report is describing only part of your
history — press Analyse to bring it up to date.

## Rebuilding from scratch

**Rebuild** throws the existing results away and re-reads every conversation from
the beginning.

Use it rarely — when you suspect an earlier run produced something odd, or after
switching to a different AI model, which may group things differently. It costs
AI calls for every conversation you have, so it is not a button to press
casually.

## Good habits

- **Press Analyse weekly**, not daily. Topics do not change fast enough to be
  worth watching hour by hour.
- **Read the gap list as a to-do list.** Writing the top three gaps is usually a
  bigger SEO win than any technical tweak.
- **After writing a page for a gap**, re-scan the knowledge base so the assistant
  can answer it. See **[Knowledge Base](/knowledge)** — a new page is invisible to
  the assistant until it has been scanned.
- **Export the conversations** monthly if you want a long-term record beyond the
  built-in report. See **[Conversations](/conversations)**.

# Conversations

One row per conversation. This is the record of what visitors actually asked —
and it is the raw material for everything on the SEO Insights tab.

![Conversations, Live Sessions tab](/img/06-conversations-live-v2.png)

## One row is one conversation

Not one message, not one session per page. A visitor who asks six questions in
one visit produces **one row**, with the whole back-and-forth stored inside it.

That makes the list readable at a glance: fifteen rows means fifteen visitors had
a conversation with you.

## The tabs

| Tab | What it is for |
| --- | --- |
| **Live Sessions** | The conversation list — read transcripts, re-send emails, delete |
| **SEO Insights** | What people ask about, and what your site has no answer for |
| **Storage** | How much space the records take, and manual cleanup |

## The columns

| Column | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| ☑ | Tick to act on several conversations at once |
| **Visitor** | The name and email from the lead form, or "Guest" if the form was off |
| **Started** | When the conversation began |
| **Turns** | How many question-and-answer exchanges took place |
| **Page** | The page the visitor was on when they started chatting |
| **Sent** | Whether the transcript email has gone out yet |
| **Actions** | See below |

**Page** is the column worth getting into the habit of reading. It answers
"which of my pages make people ask questions?", and it works even when the AI is
switched off, because it is counted straight from the record.

## The actions on each row

| Action | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **View** | Opens the full transcript — every question and answer, in order |
| **Re-send Email** | Sends that transcript to your notification address right now |
| **Delete** | Removes the conversation **and** what the assistant remembered about that visitor |

**Re-send Email** is the one to use when a visitor asks you to follow up, or when
you spot an important conversation after the automatic email has already gone.

> ⚠️ **Deleting is permanent.**
> Deleting a conversation removes the whole exchange, including the assistant's
> memory of that visitor. There is no undo, and the transcript is your only
> record of what was said. **Export first** if you might want the data.

## Working with several at once

Tick the boxes on any rows, and a bar appears with the actions available for the
selection:

- **Delete** the selected conversations
- **End and email** them — closes each session and sends its transcript

Both report back exactly what succeeded and what did not, rather than quietly
half-finishing. If you select fifteen and three fail to email, it says so.

Tick the box in the heading row to select **every conversation on the current
page**. Selection is cleared when you change page, so you can never delete more
than you can see.

## Finding things again

**Pagination** is at the bottom. Choose **10, 20, 30 or 40** conversations per
page, and the footer tells you where you are — "Showing 21–40 of 152" — so the
total is never a guess.

**Refresh** reloads the list with the latest conversations. Useful while you are
testing: ask the widget a question in another tab, press Refresh, and watch the
row appear.

## Exporting

The **Export** button at the top of the tab produces a file of your conversations
— **CSV** for a spreadsheet, or **JSONL** for feeding to an AI for analysis.

![The export dialog](/img/13-export-v2.png)

Two ways to choose what goes in:

| Option | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **By date** | You want everything in a period — a month, a campaign |
| **Last N conversations** | You want the most recent hundred, without caring when they happened |

The dialog tells you **how many conversations and roughly how much data** the
download will contain before it starts, so a large export is never a surprise.

Exports are read-only. Nothing in your database is changed by exporting, and the
file comes out one row per conversation with the full transcript in a single
column.

## Finding out what to write next

The reason to keep conversations is not nostalgia. Spend five minutes on the
**[SEO Insights](/seo-insights)** tab after a busy week: it turns this list into a
concrete answer to "what should we put on the website next?"
